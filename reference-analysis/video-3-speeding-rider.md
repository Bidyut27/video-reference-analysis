# Video 3 — "Speeding Rider" (36.7 s)

**Premise:** A cocky motorcyclist with no helmet speeds through town and is stopped by a traffic officer, who prepares a "no helmet" fine. The rider orders a helmet on a phone shopping app, and a courier delivers it instantly, so the fine is cancelled. The officer then smugly pulls out his own phone showing a **speed-radar violation**, and the rider is caught anyway. It is a **double-twist** comedy that carries a road-safety message.

## 1. Visual style

- **Art direction:** Western TV-cartoon style with **thick uniform outlines**, flat cel colours, exaggerated faces (giant eyes, huge moustache, long chin) and rubber-hose-adjacent posing. It is close in spirit to flash-era 2D TV comedy.
- **Backgrounds:** Softly blurred, painterly-to-semi-real city backdrops, so the crisp characters pop forward. The **sharp foreground character over a soft background** separation is very readable on phones.
- **Comic-book graphics:** Halftone yellow burst, radial speed lines, split-screen with coloured motion streaks, in-world text props (document, clipboard, phone UI).
- **Palette:** Teal uniform vs. navy/red rider. Each character "owns" a colour family, so the viewer instantly knows who is on screen in a sub-second cut.

## 2. Storytelling

- **Hook (0–2 s):** Action in the first frame (a wheelie) plus a motion sound, so the viewer never sees a static frame.
- **Structure:** Classic three-act gag with **two reversals**: hero in trouble → clever escape → escape undone by a bigger problem. The second reversal lands in the last 5 s, so the ending retains viewers.
- **Message:** A public-safety moral delivered through comedy rather than lecturing ("don't speed"), plus a probable **brand integration** (the shopping app/courier). This is a monetisation pattern to note. In original work, use only fictional brands or properly licensed sponsors.
- **Narration:** None. On-screen words are minimal ("SHLEM", "Jarima", "RADAR"), short enough to read in under half a second.

## 3. Animation

- **Limited animation + camera:** 31 % held frames. Many shots are a single pose with a punch-in, shake or slide. Movement concentrates on key acting beats: hand thrust, pen flourish, facial swaps (cool → shock).
- **Pose-to-pose comedy timing:** Snap between extreme expressions (no in-betweens) for maximum punch.
- **Camera:** Low angles for power (the officer, the cool rider), POV over-the-shoulder, extreme close-ups, crash zooms and small shakes on impacts.
- **FX:** Speed lines, dust, skid marks, wind on the hair, paper flaps, halftone flashes.

## 4. Audio

- **0–~11 s:** **Engine revving / wind SFX dominate.** Whisper transcribed this as "RRRRR"; it is an engine or mouth-made engine sound. A lighter music bed sits underneath.
- **~11.6 s:** A full-band, beat-driven comedy track (~144 BPM) kicks in **exactly on the split-screen face-off cut**, turning the confrontation into a "showdown".
- Comedy stingers are visible in the spectrogram around the twist (~25–30 s: rising tonal sweep, a slide-whistle-like "idea" sound), with punctuating hits on cuts.
- No speech; the mix is −14.1 LUFS.

## 5. Editing

- About 38 cuts in 36.7 s (≈1.0 s average), the fastest of the three. Many shots last 0.3–0.8 s.
- Hard cuts on the beat, split-screen as a "transition", and comic-burst cards as punctuation.
- The final reaction frame is held for ~3 s, giving viewers time to laugh and loop.

## 6. How it was likely produced

1. Character turnarounds (rider and officer) with a large **expression sheet** (cool, shocked, sad, grin, evil grin), drawn or generated with consistent-character tooling.
2. Background plates generated separately and blurred (or generated with depth of field).
3. Assembly in a 2D cut-out tool (Adobe Animate, Toon Boom Harmony, Moho, Cartoon Animator) **or** AI image-to-video for the motion shots (wheelie, near-miss), with stills plus keyframed camera for the rest.
4. Graphic cards (halftone, speed lines) from overlay packs; the split-screen built in the editor.
5. Sound design is the most labour-intensive layer: engine SFX, whooshes, stingers, the music drop-in on the face-off.

## Strongest techniques to borrow

- **Double twist** with the second reversal in the final 5 s.
- **Music entry on the conflict beat** (the first ~11 s is SFX-led, then the score arrives).
- **Colour-owned characters** for sub-second readability.
- **Expression-sheet comedy** with snap cuts between extremes.
- Comic-book cards (split-screen, halftone, speed lines) as cheap, high-energy punctuation.
- A moral or utility message wrapped in comedy, which is shareable and brand-safe.
