# Video 2 — "Bus Seat" (19.7 s)

**Premise:** A young man stands on a bus, and a younger woman sits nearby. The pink, sparkly romance staging makes the viewer (and the man) believe she is flirting. She stands up and offers him her seat, and a thought bubble reveals she sees him as an old man. The two year captions at the start, **"1999"** and **"2008"**, read as their birth years and set up the punchline that to her he is "old". It is a classic **misread-romance → generational-age twist**.

## 1. Visual style

- **Art direction:** Webtoon/manhwa-influenced 2D illustration with clean linework, soft gradient shading and expressive anime-style eyes for the woman. The man is drawn more scruffy (stubble, hairy arm, messy hair), and that design choice quietly supports the "older" punchline.
- **Lighting:** Strong **backlit window glow**, with an overexposed yellow-green exterior flaring into the interior. This is the key mood tool.
- **Colour grading as narrative:** A neutral palette in the setup → heavy **magenta/pink bloom, lens bokeh circles and twinkle stars** during the "romance" → **golden** for her innocent joy → **desaturated navy** for his despair → neutral daylight for the reveal. Colour tells the audience how to feel at every beat.
- **Typography:** Large hand-drawn-style year captions (cream for him, pink for her) at the top centre, inside the safe area.

## 2. Storytelling

- **Hook (0–3 s):** Two numbers on screen with no explanation create a curiosity gap that pays off at the end. Viewers who catch it rewatch.
- **Structure:** Setup → false lead → escalation → reveal in 20 s. It uses the **unreliable-genre** technique: dress the scene as romance, deliver comedy.
- **Emotion:** Hope, then embarrassment. The relatable cringe drives shares and tags ("this is you").
- **Narration:** None. Everything is carried by expression, colour and the final thought bubble.

## 3. Animation

- **Mostly limited animation:** 21 % of frames are holds. Shots are near-still illustrations with subtle motion: head turns, blinks, eye darts, hair sway, hand gestures, and slow camera drift/zoom.
- **Overlay FX are doing most of the "animation":** floating bokeh circles, twinkling 4-point stars and soft light leaks, composited on top. These are cheap overlay assets that make still art feel alive.
- **Depth:** Foreground blur in the over-the-shoulder shot (the man as a soft magenta silhouette) fakes a shallow depth-of-field camera.
- **Reaction cards:** Full-frame emotion close-ups with symbolic backgrounds (sparkle burst, gloom lines) are standard comedy-anime shorthand.

## 4. Audio

- A single **continuous music track** for the whole video (steady beat visible across the spectrogram, ~78 BPM, or ≈156 counted as half-time), starting at about 0.5 s. No speech and little distinct SFX.
- The audio does **not** change at the reveal. The comedy relies purely on the visual twist, with the trending-style song acting as the emotional "romance" frame. For original work, adding a record-scratch or music cut at the reveal would sharpen it (see the [comparison](comparison.md)).
- Mix: −14.1 LUFS.

## 5. Editing

- About 15 shots in 19.7 s (≈1.3 s average). The cut rhythm accelerates around 10–12 s (four cuts in under a second) just before the reveal, which builds tension through edit speed.
- Hard cuts. The colour/FX layer changes on cuts, not via transitions.
- Ends right on the punchline frame, with no outro. That encourages loops and rewatches.

## 6. How it was likely produced

1. Character sheets for both leads (consistent face, outfit, colours), generated with an image model using a reference/character-consistency feature, or drawn.
2. Per-shot illustrations (≈10 unique images) at 9:16.
3. Subtle motion through image-to-video (prompted "subtle head turn, blink, hair sway") **or** puppet/mesh deformation (Live2D, After Effects Puppet tool).
4. Grade and overlays in CapCut or After Effects: pink glow, bokeh/sparkle stock overlays in Screen/Add blend mode, light leaks.
5. Caption text, music, export.

## Strongest techniques to borrow

- A **number/label hook** that only makes sense at the end, which drives rewatches.
- **Colour grading as an emotion switch** per beat.
- Overlay FX (bokeh, sparkles, light leaks) to make near-still art feel premium.
- Accelerating the cut rhythm before the reveal.
- The **genre bait-and-switch** (romance staging → comedy payoff).
